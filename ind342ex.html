Short Notes

Observations

Shorter reflections on language, attention, and what happens when clinical habits and digital habits begin to blur.

May 2026

A colleague described a difficult conversation with a patient's family. Afterwards, she said: "I kept thinking about how I could have framed it better." Not said differently. Not explained more clearly. Framed. The language of prompts has entered the language of care without anyone announcing it.

May 2026

There is a specific discomfort that arrives when you ask someone a question and immediately begin composing your follow-up. You are no longer listening. You are processing. The person in front of you has become an input. Most of us do not notice when this happens.

March 2026

Grief is inefficient. It does not produce output. A mind trained for ten hours a day to reduce inefficiency will, eventually, begin to apply that training to grief. The results appear in clinical settings much later — when the person arrives and says: I should have cried by now. I don't know where it went.

About

A Clinician Who Writes

I am a General Practitioner with more than twenty years of clinical service across hospital medicine, primary care, and health administration in Sri Lanka. I am currently completing an MD in Health Informatics as a Batch 9 trainee at the Postgraduate Institute of Medicine, University of Colombo.

My writing grows from that clinical background — from what I have seen in wards, in outpatient rooms, in health information system implementations, and in the space between what digital health promises and what it delivers on the ground.

I write here about AI in healthcare, digital health, and what technology quietly does to the people who use it and the people they care for.

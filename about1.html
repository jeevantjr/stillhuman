I am a medical doctor working at the intersection of healthcare, information systems, and human experience. My professional background spans clinical medicine, public health information systems, and health informatics. I hold qualifications in MBBS, MCGP, and MSc Biomedical Informatics, and I am currently an MD Trainee in Health Informatics at the Postgraduate Institute of Medicine, University of Colombo. Much of my work takes place inside systems — health data platforms, digital health programs, governance frameworks, and emerging applications of artificial intelligence. I believe technology can strengthen healthcare, but only when it remains accountable to the people it serves. Over the years, however, I became interested in a different question. What happens when the habits we develop around technology begin to shape the way we think, remember, grieve, decide, and relate to one another? After years of working with AI tools, refining prompts, analysing data, and building information systems, I began noticing subtle changes in everyday life. Not dramatic changes. Small ones. The way a conversation becomes an optimisation problem. The way certainty is borrowed from a screen. The way efficiency quietly replaces presence. Those observations eventually became my first English book, *Are You Still Human?* (2026) — a short literary work built around three true stories and one recurring question. This website is a home for those explorations. Here I write about memory, artificial intelligence, family, healthcare, identity, and the small human moments that technology cannot fully explain. Not because technology is the problem. But because remaining human may become one of the most important skills of the AI age. If these questions interest you, welcome. — Dr. T.

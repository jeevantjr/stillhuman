1. First Impression

What message does the website communicate within the first 10 seconds?

Within the first 10 seconds, a visitor landing on drjeevaraj.com sees a book cover, the words "Still Human," and the description "Author, Digital Health Researcher." The dominant visual message is literary author, not health informatics professional. A recruiter visiting from LinkedIn expecting a digital health or AI specialist will need to scroll significantly before finding the technical and clinical credentials that make this profile exceptional.

Does it appear professional and trustworthy?

Yes — the design is genuinely impressive. The website is custom-coded (HTML/CSS/JS), responsive, well-structured, and visually consistent. The typography, colour palette, and layout communicate a high level of digital literacy. A recruiter notices immediately that this person built their own professional website — which is itself a differentiator in the health informatics space. Trust markers are present: SLMC registration number, institutional affiliations, peer-reviewed BMJ Open publication, World Bank recognition, and a State Literary Award. However, the homepage buries these under the book promotional content.

Key issue: The homepage is optimised for a literary audience, not for international recruiters or consulting clients. The professional identity is strong but it is hidden behind the author identity.

2. Remote Work Readiness

Would you consider the owner for remote roles?

Yes, with strong potential. The portfolio page explicitly states "Available for remote collaboration worldwide." Digital skills are clearly demonstrated through 8 deployed projects, active GitHub profiles, and a working knowledge of modern web technologies. The AI Reviewer positioning is particularly well-suited to remote work — companies such as Scale AI, Surge AI, Appen, and health-focused AI firms actively recruit clinical reviewers remotely.

What types of remote jobs would be suitable?

  • Clinical AI Content Reviewer — validating medical AI outputs for companies like Scale AI, Appen, iMerit Health
  • Health Informatics Consultant — DHIS2 implementation, digital health strategy, HIS governance
  • Digital Health Advisor — NGOs, WHO, UNICEF, MSF, PATH
  • Medical Writer / Reviewer — healthcare content, clinical documentation, AI-generated health content QA
  • Remote Research Collaborator — international health research projects (as demonstrated by BMJ Open)
  • Online Health Informatics Trainer — DHIS2 training, public health data analytics
  • Freelance Health IT Developer — PWA and public health tools for NGOs and governments

What concerns might a recruiter have?

  • Availability conflict: Currently in a full-time MD Trainee programme (PGIM). Recruiters will ask: can he actually take on remote work while in training?
  • No stated availability hours: No mention of how many hours per week are available for remote roles
  • No testimonials from remote clients: The AI Reviewer and consulting capability is claimed but not evidenced by client feedback
  • LinkedIn URL inconsistency: CV page links to linkedin.com/in/geevanathy while portfolio and homepage link to linkedin.com/in/drjeevaraj — this looks careless to a recruiter
  • No rate card or pricing: Consulting potential is stated but no indication of fees or project types

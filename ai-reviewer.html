What I Review

Clinical competencies applied to AI evaluation

Core Competency

Clinical Reasoning

Evaluating whether AI diagnostic and therapeutic chains-of-thought reflect accepted clinical logic — ICD-11, DSM-5, WHO protocols. Identifying departures from standard of care that no NLP training alone can catch.

Core Competency

Pharmacology & Drug Safety

Verifying drug interactions, dosage thresholds, contraindications, and treatment guidelines against BNF, WHO formularies, and primary literature. Critical for LLMs generating prescribing advice.

Core Competency

Hallucination Detection

Identifying confident-but-wrong outputs — fabricated drug doses, invented clinical guidelines, incorrect diagnostic criteria. Built from 20 years of knowing when a clinical statement is subtly off.

Core Competency

Biomedical Annotation

Structured annotation of clinical datasets using SNOMED CT, LOINC, ICD-10/11 hierarchies. MSc Biomedical Informatics provides the ontological backbone for high-quality, consistent labelling.

Core Competency

Medical Protocol Validation

Cross-referencing AI outputs against national and international clinical guidelines, treatment protocols, and emerging evidence — ensuring AI stays aligned with current practice, not textbook editions.

Core Competency

Health Data & Informatics

Review of AI outputs in EHR, DHIS2, and public health data contexts. A decade of designing real digital health systems means I evaluate AI-generated health data outputs with end-to-end deployment in mind.

Why a Physician

What clinical experience brings to AI evaluation that informatics training alone cannot

Bedside Pattern Recognition

Twenty years of clinical decision-making produce pattern recognition for subtle errors — the kind of diagnostic drift that passes automated checks but would alarm an attending physician.

Risk Calibration

Clinical practice requires constant risk-benefit weighing under uncertainty. This transfers directly to grading AI error severity — distinguishing cosmetic inaccuracies from patient-safety failures.

Real-World Context

Textbook correct is not always clinically correct. I evaluate AI through the lens of real Sri Lankan public health settings, resource-limited environments, and diverse patient populations.

Technical Fluency

MSc Biomedical Informatics and 8 deployed digital health systems mean I write feedback that is simultaneously medically accurate and technically actionable for engineering teams.

Research Standards

Published in BMJ Open and the Jaffna Medical Journal. Evidence-based medicine discipline means I evaluate AI claims against primary literature, not secondary summaries.

Global South Perspective

AI trained predominantly on Western data needs review from clinicians practicing in different epidemiological and resource contexts. Sri Lanka and APAC are not edge cases — they are the majority.

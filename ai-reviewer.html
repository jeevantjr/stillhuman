What I do

Clinical AI Evaluation

I review AI-generated medical content with the critical eye of a practicing physician. That means I am not checking text against text — I am checking AI reasoning against twenty years of clinical reality.

What I review What I check it against
Clinical reasoning chains — differential diagnosis, triage logic, referral decisions ICD-11 protocols, WHO clinical guidelines, 20 years of front-line diagnostic practice
Pharmacological content — dosing, interactions, contraindications, drug safety BNF, WHO Model Formulary, peer-reviewed literature, oncology practice at NCCP
Public health data and statements — epidemiology, surveillance, NCD statistics Ministry of Health Sri Lanka datasets, DHIS2 records, WHO surveillance reports
Medical text annotation — labelling clinical notes, pathology, coded data SNOMED CT, LOINC, ICD-10/11, DSM-5, clinical ontologies (MSc Biomedical Informatics)
AI hallucinations — factual errors, invented citations, plausible-but-wrong claims Clinical knowledge base, primary sources, structured fact-checking methodology
Patient safety guardrails — outputs that could mislead a clinician or patient 20+ years identifying how clinical errors occur and what makes them dangerous

Identifying and flagging medical hallucinations — confident AI outputs that are factually incorrect, clinically unsafe, or misleading. Creating high-fidelity safety annotations that protect patients and build safer clinical AI systems.

Why a physician

What a Clinician Sees That a Data Scientist Cannot

Medical AI review done by someone without clinical training catches errors in text. Done by a physician, it catches errors in reasoning — which is where the dangerous ones live.

🏥

Pattern recognition from the bedside

Five years in a Medical ICU managing 80–100 inpatients builds a clinical intuition that no dataset can replicate. When an AI presents a plausible but wrong differential diagnosis, I recognise it from experience, not from looking it up.

⚠️

Knowledge of how clinical errors cause harm

I have seen what happens when dosing errors, missed diagnoses, and incorrect contraindications reach patients. That lived knowledge gives my safety evaluations a different weight — I am not just flagging technical inaccuracies, I am flagging patient harm risks.

🔬

Research-trained critical appraisal

Co-authorship on papers in BMJ Open (University of Michigan collaboration) and the Jaffna Medical Journal means I read AI medical citations the way I read research — checking methodology, population specificity, and whether the evidence actually supports the claim.

💻

Informatics context for AI outputs

Having built eight digital health systems and studied biomedical informatics at postgraduate level, I understand how AI outputs are consumed downstream — by systems, workflows, and clinicians — and I write evaluations that engineering teams can act on, not just medical teams.
